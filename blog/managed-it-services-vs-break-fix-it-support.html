Choosing an IT support model

Managed IT services vs break-fix IT support

Both models can solve problems, but they assign responsibility, risk and improvement work very differently.

Published 15 July 2026 · BizIT Solutions

Break-fix support is reactive: the business requests help when something stops working or a change is needed. Managed IT is ongoing: a provider takes responsibility for an agreed set of users, devices, cloud services and recurring tasks. The better option depends on how heavily your business relies on technology and how much internal capability you already have.

How break-fix support works

You pay for time or a defined job. It can be sensible for a microbusiness with a simple environment and someone internally who can manage accounts, suppliers and routine checks. The trade-off is that documentation, patching, access reviews and long-term fixes may receive attention only after disruption occurs.

How managed IT services work

A managed agreement defines supported systems and ongoing responsibilities. It may combine user support with monitoring, maintenance, Microsoft 365 administration, onboarding, security improvements, backup oversight and supplier coordination. Because the relationship is continuous, recurring problems and planned improvements can be managed alongside urgent requests.

Questions that reveal the right fit

  • Does downtime immediately affect revenue or customer service?
  • Who checks backups, access, patching and Microsoft 365 settings?
  • Is there current documentation for accounts, devices and suppliers?
  • Do repeat issues consume management time?
  • Does the business need an IT roadmap or only occasional repairs?

A hybrid approach may work

Some businesses retain project-based support but schedule regular reviews for security, backup and account administration. Others use managed support for core users and systems while scoping larger migrations separately. The important point is to make ownership explicit so important tasks do not sit between the business and its vendors.
